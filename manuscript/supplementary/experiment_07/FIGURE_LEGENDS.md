# Supplementary figure legends

**Supplementary Fig. S1 | Search-led method discovery and later bibliographic audit.** The upper panel follows the prespecified September 2026 database search, initial citation discovery, reviewed records and method-level consolidation. The original freeze comprised 247 identities (218 retained); a separate 7 October source audit added four methods, yielding 251 identities (222 retained: three direct, 27 near-direct, 192 supporting). The lower panel separates the blind-safe bibliographic recovery flow from an independently prioritised 1,881-record scope review yielding 48 provisional families; neither population was added to the method-level landscape. The first citation-search wave was completed, but saturation under the iterative stopping rule has not been demonstrated.

**Supplementary Fig. S2 | Publication identity does not establish abstract identity.** Eight mutually exclusive request-level classes account for all 5,173 bibliographic recovery tasks. Requested DOI/version identities were reconciled against OpenAlex records and independently registered Crossref or DataCite DOI/title evidence. Even an exact DOI/title registration match does not authenticate the abstract attached to an aggregated OpenAlex work as belonging to the requested publication version. No aggregate abstract was automatically promoted.

**Supplementary Fig. S3 | Different phylogenetic methods can estimate different quantities.** The illustrated A→G→A trajectory occurs along *one* branch and is conceptual, not an inferred history from this study. Parent and child both carry A, giving zero net endpoint change despite two substitutions. Ancestral-state, endpoint-change, event-history and homoplasy outputs therefore require distinct scoring targets. This illustration does not establish JC69 compatibility or benchmark eligibility for any candidate software.

**References:** Nielsen R. Mapping mutations on phylogenies. *Systematic Biology* (2002), doi:10.1080/10635150290102393; Bollback JP. SIMMAP: stochastic character mapping of discrete traits on phylogenies. *BMC Bioinformatics* (2006), doi:10.1186/1471-2105-7-88.
